Dear Henry,

The day I met you, we played some game in Gussie's room whose name I can't remember. I was prompted to say something I admired about you, and, having just met you, the only thing I could confidently say was that I liked your deep voice. Little did I know that over the course of the next four years, you would become so much more than a deep voice to me.

Although I didn't say it on that first day, even then I had an inkling that our senses of humor were well matched. You and I share a love for a certain kind of banter that involves commanding a space like this

and riffing several consecutive times on the same one bit. This is a style of charisma I first learned from my dad, and to have a fellow banter-lover as a best friend for four years at college was a dream come true. I returned home even better than my dad at his own game. Student to master pipeline type shit.

Soon into our friendship I also learned that you were experienced in creating impressive projects with little apparent purpose. You came with a laundry list of high-effort pranks you pulled off in high school, and shit like this program that turned your camera intake into some beads or something?

Idk maybe this one was actually for a class or something. I wondered how this mischievous creativity would manifest during your time at Carleton.

You absolutely provided your fair share of pranks (twin xl bed with complete bedding and red mountain bike (Northfield)), but what I found was that your creativity brought to life some of my most unforgettable memories at Carleton. 100% of the famous Staus party gear was yours. 95% of the forest rave gear was yours, too, minus, of course, the mysterious generator that always appeared when you needed it. And I'm forever indebted to you for pulling together the first rendition of the band in Freshman Spring. After years of musical theater and a cappella in high school, I was ready to use my talents for something actually cool. Without you, I genuinely might've been tabling in Sayles for the Singing Knights Singing Telegrams. Terrible.

I don't know how far along on your journey you'll be when this letter reaches you. By now you'll probably be able to hold full conversations with me in Spanish, and you'll have grown out your hair into some unkempt dreadlocks. Maybe you'll even be far enough along to be able to explain what your project actually is. Wherever you are, and whatever you're doing, I hope that your creativity keeps on being your guiding light. It's what's made me and so many others admire you so much. Call me when you get this, I'd love to catch up.

Hugs and kisses, bro, hugs and kisses,

Cyrus
