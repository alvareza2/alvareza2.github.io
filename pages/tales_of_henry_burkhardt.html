"The journey of a thousand miles begins with a single step."
Lao Tzu

I

It is a stormy night, thunder roaring through the trees, bolts galloping from up above. The wind a howling cacophony screeching in the streets. My eyes jut upwards in front of me, before me stands a house, lit only by the flashes above. My eyes wander upwards, chalk stains the sidewalk. Four columns loom in the porch, a chair swing tottering, blown about by the wind. I've seen this house before, 4533 York Avenue S. A chill runs down my spine, as if someone is watching. My eyes jolt towards the top left window on the second floor. A face is pressed up to the glass. I meet his steely gaze, hat sitting tall on his head, an aura of authority emanating through the glass. He opens his mouth. I can't read lips but the word that leaves them is unmistakable. “Run.” Terror fills my heart, my limbs feel heavy as if filled with lead. Then it appears, the winds pick up, now a roiling vortex of air, the inky black sky now infused with a thin veil of green, like a bay leaf covered in black beans. A funnel descends ripping into the ground, the earth shakes creating a deafening roar that swallows all other noise. I move to run, but just as I do so an explosion, blasts me from my feet. The sky lights up, brighter than the day, the inky sky now transformed into a roiling inferno. The tornado now cloaked in flame…. I awake to my own scream, my covers drenched in sweat. I breathe a sigh of relief. It was only a dream. Or was it. Just as soon as my heart rate had slowed, I heard the sounds of thunder. Could it be, was my dream reality. Henry Burkhardt, he was the herald warning in my vision, I must check with him. I rush to my phone, opening snapchat. My skin becomes clammy, sweat cooling and oozing from my pores. My eyes must be deceiving me, I blink. Once. Twice. My eyes refocus, but the image on my screen remains. A tornado looking back at me on Henry's story. I race up the stairs, I must warn my family. I reach the top step but before I can say a word, my phone chimes. A notification. I look downwards in horror. A new notification from Henry Burkhardt. My hands, now shaking in fear, unlock my phone. Looking back at me, the spawn of my nightmares, the torrential vortex of flame, a tornado of fire. My body goes limp, my eyelids now heavy, I fall backwards down the stairs.

II

The sea is roiling, a thick current of foam, washing ashore. The water the color of the darkest depths, an invite to the locker. The sky, a vortex of ash, grey, dark, brooding. The shore is empty save for a few boats docked in the harbor, the bandshell is cleared, everyone knows to stay away from the seas this day. An ominous tone is set, but an ominous day it is, for it is the day of war, the Water Wars. I peer out into the sea, as my boat steers its way to the dock, the stage is set. My captain awaits me aboard, hat perched tall on his head, purple shorts and a graphic tee. Oh Capitan, My Captain, I would follow him to the ends of the earth. The wind grips our sail, and sends out onto the open sea. My heart sinks to the bottom of my torso, fear grips my lungs. I see the sails into the distance, gleaming off the roiling sea. An uneasy feeling sweeps through the boat, the smell of fear is strong. But I cannot smell it from one. My Capitan. His voice fills the air, “There is no folly of the beast of the seas which is not infinitely outdone by the madness of man.” The clouds part, sun beams lighting the churning abyss in a bright blue glow. We cut through the water with a purpose now, speed increasing, four, five, six knots. Cannons now loaded in hand, the first boat approaches. It approaches the starboard side, but before we can even react, a volley of projectiles flies through the air. Instantly we are hit, my weapons fall to my side as I am blasted. More of us are hit. Wounded, we retreat. I look around, “So much destruction, what can we do against such reckless hate.” I turn towards my capitan, his hand still firmly on the rudder, “Ride out, ride out and meet them.” He says. I grimace, “Yes, for honor and glory.” He turns back towards me, “No, for Minneapolis, for your people.” He spins the rudder facing us back toward the other ships. Now engrossed by the vitalizing words of my captain I am ready to face the day. My hands no longer shake in fear, my feet now firmly planted on the ground of the deck. We roar through the waves, the spirit of the seas and the winds on our side. We splash over a wave as we reach the next ship, spraying its deck with water, and then unload onto the ship, soaking all passengers aboard. But to my horror, one shot flies back through the air, spiraling, directly at my captain. My legs as if autonomous propel me through the air, I block the shot, but in the process throw myself overboard. I hit the seas, and begin to sink towards the watery depth.

III

When I close my eyes at night, I can only see his face. The only students to evade my grasp, my biggest failing as a professional. I lean back in my chair, before me lays a map of Carleton college. This is a place in disarray, ruffians roam the halls, chaos grips the campus. At the center of it lies one man. Henry Burkhardt. He has terrorised the school for 3 years thus far, I'm sure of it, but the only piece of evidence I have of his crimes lies before me. The generator. I know what he's done, and I know what he will do, but only I have the willpower to stop him. The radio crackles, a voice over the phone, “Go to Canoe House.” I spring into action, whatever is happening, it must be the work of Henry Burkhardt. I knock on the door, ordering the students out of the house, “Parties over.” They run like bugs when turning over a rock. I begin my investigation, the words wash over me, “Floor about to collapse… Bands preforming… Mucket… Henry.” This is my chance to finally catch him. I call in, “More units needed, watch the exits, watch the windows.” He's here somewhere. I begin my interrogation, there's no honor among thieves, one of them will rat. But alas, no luck, no one will tell me the truth, no one will obey the law. I walk outside, eyes searching the bushes, maybe he's hiding out here. A shadow moves on the roof, my eyes lock on, it's too dark to see, but it must be him. I run over but just as fast as the shadow appears, it disappears, the only thing on the roof, a bag of trail mix. I turn in defeat, that must have been him. As the night continues, the opportunity slips away, another failure, another failure caused by him. I return home, walk into the living room and sink into my chair. One day I'll catch him, I mutter to myself.

But alas the day never came, as the months changed and the days grew longer, no more opportunities presented themselves. As graduation passed, I realized that the day would never come, crime had no meaning to me anymore. I arrive on the scene of a disturbance, but these students make it too easy, they don't have the same wit. Without Henry, justice has no purpose.
